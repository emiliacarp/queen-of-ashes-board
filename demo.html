The dancingSix shots re-shot for movement

  • The note was that nobody was really dancing. Measured against the SENSE8 reference Craig showed us — 2.40 accents a second, an accent being a hit the body lands on — twelve of sixteen court shots were under it, and the busiest shots scored the fewest accents. The movement was in the camera, not the bodies.
  • Six were re-shot with the camera locked and the choreography written as hit → dead-still hold → hit, naming the exact body part. The originals are all still banked and still usable.
  • B05 0.50 → 2.32 · A02 0.66 → 1.66 · F01 0.00 → 1.49 · E01 0.00 → 1.16 · E03 0.66 → 1.16 · B02 0.66 → 0.83.
What the six taught us: the plate has to be able to do the move. Naming a body part that has room to snap works. Asking men packed shoulder to shoulder to drop into a lunge does not — that is B02, and it is the one that barely moved.

Still openFour pairs still read as the same picture

  • Measured on the finished cut, not the plates: B05 / C07 / F01 (aisle wides), B06 / C02 (mirror hall), C03 / C06 (corridor), B01 / I02 (him, medium).
  • Two of those sit close together in time and are the ones worth fixing — B06 / C02 are 9 seconds apart, C03 / C06 are 14. The other two pairs are half a minute or more apart and read as rhyme, not repeat.
  • One was caught and fixed during this pass: the reveal and the walk-out were the same picture two bars apart, so the walk-out was rebuilt looking in through the bronze doors from the dark hallway. That is the shot now.
Same structural cause as before — one plate feeding several shots. The fix is another room or another angle for the second half of each pair, not a re-frame of the same take.
