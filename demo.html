How to watch itWhat is real and what is not

  • All 45 shots are finished footage now — including the whole ending, which was slates last time: the room folding over, a hundred hands going to a hundred masks, the reveal, her bare face, the doorway, the walk out, dawn.
  • Every shot is cut to its bar length and sits on its real beat. Nothing is a placeholder.
  • No grade, no titles, no sound design. Song only.

The dancingSix shots re-shot for movement

  • The note was that nobody was really dancing. Measured against the SENSE8 reference Craig showed us — 2.40 accents a second, an accent being a hit the body lands on — twelve of sixteen court shots were under it, and the busiest shots scored the fewest accents. The movement was in the camera, not the bodies.
  • Six were re-shot with the camera locked and the choreography written as hit → dead-still hold → hit, naming the exact body part. The originals are all still banked and still usable.
  • B05 0.50 → 2.32 · A02 0.66 → 1.66 · F01 0.00 → 1.49 · E01 0.00 → 1.16 · E03 0.66 → 1.16 · B02 0.66 → 0.83.
What the six taught us: the plate has to be able to do the move. Naming a body part that has room to snap works. Asking men packed shoulder to shoulder to drop into a lunge does not — that is B02, and it is the one that barely moved.

New roomsThe steel box, three times

  • Everything in this film was marble and glass, which is exactly why the same picture kept coming back. Three shots now live in a room made of something else: a brushed steel box three metres across, one strip of light, and her alone in it.
  • C02 is the first phrase — liquid spine against a snapping head, left arm pinned, only the right glove moving. C06 is the second — both hands travelling slowly down over the gown, shoulders turning away. C07 is the close — one gloved hand rising to her neck as her head turns off the lens.
  • The helipad and the concrete stairwell were built and shot, then cut: the masks that came back had hollow black eye sockets instead of visible eyes, which reads horror rather than masquerade. Both takes are kept, neither is in the film.
Measured on the finished cut: four look-alike families down to one, and the pair that is left sits 76 seconds apart, which reads as a rhyme rather than a repeat. The three steel-box shots do not read as each other — the scale changes too much between them.

The manRecast, and shot like a photograph

  • He read as a generic AI man, and an A/B proved the cause was the photography, not the face: dead-centre, flat frontal light, and a background as sharp as he was.
  • He is now off-centre and three-quarters turned, lit hard from one side with the far side of his face in shadow, at f/2 so the room behind him is genuinely soft — plus real skin, real asymmetry, and something always slightly out of place.
  • He is also about fifteen years younger, with the beard down to stubble. All six shots that read his face were re-shot.
