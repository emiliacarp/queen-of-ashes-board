Pick one of sixThe woman — the queen of the house

She wears the one gold filigree half-mask; her mouth stays free. Black silk, long opera gloves, sleek dark hair, deep red lips — the only colour in the entire film. She takes the mask off only at the end. Whichever face is chosen becomes the film's single female identity: the queen, and every one of the hundred faces in the last chorus.

The courtThe masked men

Venetian bauta masks: matte bone-white, heavy brow, angular nose, a strong squared jaw, no mouth, eyes lost in shadow, a fine gold edge — and their own hair visible above the mask, so they read as men in masks rather than blank heads. Black tie throughout.

The courtThe women — and the reveal

The women of the house are unmasked, identical, expressionless: tuxedo jacket over a white shirt, sleek hair, red lips. Who they are is the film's question. The last chorus answers it — every mask in the room comes off and every face underneath is hers.
